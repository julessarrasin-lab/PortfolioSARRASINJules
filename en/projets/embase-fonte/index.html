Context

This project is SAÉ 2.01 at the IUT of Aix-en-Provence (a university learning and assessment project), carried out in a pair with Ahmet Kapar.

We started from a part supplied in CATIA: a cast-iron base (EN-GJS-400). The task was to design its shape so that it can be cast, with the dimensions given in the specification. So I had to think about manufacturing at the same time as the shape.

2

My part of the work

I did the CATIA work, the definition drawings, the justifications of the choices and the report, together with Ahmet.

3

The casting choices

All these choices follow the foundry course.

  • Parting line (the surface where the two mould halves separate): it was the only workable position. It also makes it easier to remove the core.
  • A single core, for the Ø40 bore. The four other bores are too small to have a core.
  • Machining allowances: extra material added on the surfaces that will be machined afterwards.
  • Radii of 1 mm on the edges and 3 mm on the junctions, from the table in the course.
  • Draft angles: slightly inclined walls so that the part can be removed from the mould.
4

From the raw part to the finished part

Between the two, the surface finish and cleanliness change. Casting is not precise: that is why the raw part has machining allowances, which are then machined away.

Difficulty and result

At first, I had not understood how casting works. I had to learn it in order to draw a part that can be cast.
