Context

This is my final-year STI2D project. The topic was set by the teachers: turn a mountain bike into a Solex-style moped, that is, motorise it with a hoverboard wheel.

There were two of us on the team and I was the project manager. That meant organising the work and making decisions. The idea of disengaging the motor, which is the core of the system, was planned from the start.

2

What the project had to do

  • Motorise a mountain bike with a hoverboard wheel, with speed limited to 25 km/h.
  • Program a progressive acceleration and an automatic deceleration above 25 km/h.
  • Design a mechanical linkage that disengages the motor: the bike becomes a normal bike again, only slightly heavier.

25 km/h is the legal limit for this kind of vehicle. It was imposed by the teachers, through the Arduino code that controls the motor.

3

The mechanical linkage

The principle is simple to describe. A cylindrical part surrounds the seat tube. It is linked to a connecting rod, which is itself linked to another connecting rod. When the cylinder is raised, it lifts the wheel support: the motor then detaches from the wheel and the bike becomes an ordinary bike again.

Another idea had been considered: an easily removable motor. It was dropped, because a motor like this is not practical to carry in a backpack.

My part of the work

  • Project manager: sharing the work with my teammate and deciding when a choice had to be made.
  • SolidWorks modelling: I modelled all the parts, in three successive versions.
  • 3D printing: the modelled parts were 3D printed to be fitted on the bike.
  • Arduino: I limited myself to declaring the variables in the code.
6

The difficulties

The wheel support. It is fixed on two screw holes that were originally meant for a mudguard. I had to find a good fit: the support must not break under load, while staying within the allowed space.

The voltage. The teachers had handed out boards to be programmed for 12 V, but they supplied a 24 V battery. Buying new equipment cost money that we did not have.

A project manager's decision. The hardest decision was to give up the portable, battery-powered system and run the bike only on mains power. We simply plugged it in with a transformer.

7

Looking back

Before starting, I thought the code would be the hardest part. That is what happened.

The project was fun. But the bike was a wreck that we had to repair, and most of the equipment was of poor quality. I think a project like this should be done with more resources, or at least with equipment that fits together.

What I take from it: I learned a lot about 3D printing and 3D modelling. This project gave me a taste for mechanics and rapid prototyping, and pushed me to continue in mechanical engineering.
