Can gripper

A gripper that picks up a can without marking it, for a 5-axis milling machine. Project in progress.

Context

A mechanical design project carried out in a team of four: Jarod, Giuliano, Adam and me. It started in mid-September and ends in March.

The topic was chosen by a coin toss between two ideas: a catapult and a gripper.

2

The need

The gripper must pick up 33 cl cans, standard or slim, full or empty, wet or dry. The work is shared between two machines: the 5-axis milling machine lifts the can over a wall about 20 cm high, and the gripper holds it during that movement.

  • Do not mark the can.
  • Keep it upright.
  • A single button to operate the gripper.
  • Quick mounting.
  • Battery power.
3

Technology watch

Before drawing anything, we compared six existing types of gripper to see which ones could meet the need.

Type of gripperVerdict
Suction cupExcluded The specification forbids gripping the can by its metal bottom.
MagneticExcluded A layer of acrylic makes the can non-magnetic.
AngularSuitable
IrisSuitable Considered then dropped: gears more complex than our gripper.
DeformableSuitable
TongsSuitable

We also studied patent EP1336533A2, which was not suitable.

4

The chosen solution

We chose a gripper that uses the weight of the load to tighten more or less strongly. This principle is used on building sites and in machining to carry cylinders and heavy parts. We have never seen it in a three-jaw version.

How it works: by gravity, the jaws close on the can. A cylinder (actuator) is used to open them again. The idea of the mechanism is mine.

5

My part of the work

  • 3D modelling in CATIA: the model is used to understand the mechanism.
  • Mechanical study: friction coefficient, load carried by an empty can without deforming it.
  • Jaws: several types of jaws modelled and 3D printed, to fit different diameters.

I often work in a pair with Giuliano.

6

Where the project stands

The project is in progress and is not finished: the CATIA model is used to understand the mechanism, but the actuator and the sizing are not yet settled.

The main risk is that the gripping surface is too slippery to hold the can. The next step is a test on the milling machine.

7

What I am learning

Team management, getting to grips with CATIA, and finding test methods, for example to check the load the gripper can hold.
